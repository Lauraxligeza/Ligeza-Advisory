Why international customers may understand an offer and remain unconvinced

Comprehension is only one part of a purchase decision. The necessary evidence depends on the offer and the context in which it is evaluated.

Ligeza Advisory Insights

Commercial question

When target market visitors do not act, is the problem comprehension, relevance, evidence, commercial fit or friction elsewhere in the journey?

Why it matters

A translated page can be easy to understand and still leave important questions unanswered. This does not mean that all buyers in a country share one fixed definition of credibility. A procurement team considering enterprise software and a consumer buying a low cost subscription face different risks, even when they are in the same city.

Evidence

A useful review considers several variables together:

  • Category: conventions and proof vary between software, travel, healthcare and professional services.
  • Price: greater financial commitment may require more detailed justification.
  • Segment: individuals, procurement teams and channel partners evaluate different concerns.
  • Competitive environment: the standard set by relevant alternatives affects what appears complete.
  • Purchasing risk: operational, legal, reputational and switching risks change the information needed.
  • Market evidence: sales feedback, journey data, interviews and competitor analysis should test the initial hypothesis.

Analysis

Low conversion is an observation, not a diagnosis. Missing product detail, weak differentiation, an unsuitable price, poor traffic quality, unfamiliar payment options or a difficult handover can all reduce response. Nationality alone explains none of these.

The most responsible approach is to map where visitors stop, identify the questions that remain unresolved and compare the experience with credible alternatives in the same category. That creates hypotheses that can be tested.

Limitations and uncertainties

Analytics shows behaviour but not motive. Interviews provide explanations but may not predict action. Competitor conventions can indicate a market baseline, but copying them may erase useful differentiation. Findings should therefore state the evidence used, alternative explanations and the confidence placed in each conclusion.

Practical implications

  • Segment performance data before attributing the problem to the whole market.
  • Review the offer and price alongside the wording.
  • List the evidence each important claim provides.
  • Check operational details such as payment, delivery, support and returns.
  • Test the highest priority uncertainty before redesigning the full journey.

What to examine next

Choose one important journey and document the audience, promise, required commitment, current evidence and points of friction. Compare this with research from the target segment, then decide what to change or test.
